# M2 Single-player race loop: handoff note

Branch `m2-race-loop`. Built and verified in a GPU-less cloud container (4 cores, 15 GB RAM,
Ubuntu 24.04, Mesa lavapipe) on 2026-10-27. No dependency was added or upgraded.

## What was built

| Item | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| City track data | `data/tracks/downtown-loop/track.toml` | 2.14 km greybox street circuit: 14 corner nodes in driving order, each with corner radius, road width, apron (run-off) per side, elevation, banking and curbs. Eight 90° grid corners, a 470 m main avenue, a fast 18° kink at 150 km/h, a banked 11 m hairpin, a 6 m overpass on a 10 % grade, walls on both sides everywhere (an escape lane at turn 1). Race layout: start line, 8-slot staggered grid, 3 sectors, a checkpoint every 90 m (24), sprint finish. |
| Track compiler | `crates/racer-trackc` (library + `racer-trackc validate/compile`) | Builds the centreline from the nodes (straights joined by circular fillets), samples it every metre with a banked 3D frame, generates road, apron, curb (red/white), wall (inner face, top, outer face down to the ground) and marking meshes, grid poses, checkpoints and greybox buildings. Validation: value ranges, fillets that fit between nodes, inner walls that cannot fold in tight corners, grades ≤ 12 %, banking ≤ 10°, no self-overlap of the corridor, a straight long enough for the grid, sector/start/finish points on the route. `compile` writes `generated/`: the racing line, every car's point-mass model and speed profile, and a summary. |
| Racing line | `racer-trackc/src/line.rs` | Minimum curvature: coordinate descent with a Newton step per offset on the discrete integral of curvature squared (turning angle² / length), inside the corridor the road leaves a car (half width + 0.3 m, wheels may use 0.5 m of curb), from a 32 m to a 1 m sample stride. |
| Car models and speed profiles | `racer-trackc/src/profile.rs` | Point-mass model per car measured on the `racer-dynamics` testbed: sustained lateral grip (skidpad), ABS braking (100–0), drive acceleration per 1 m/s band from a launch-control full-throttle run (shift gaps, TCS, wheel/engine inertia, rolling resistance included), drag and lift from the aero fit. Profile: corner speed from curvature, forward pass with drive inside the traction circle, backward pass with braking inside the friction ellipse, grade included. |
| Race rules | `crates/racer-sim/src/rules.rs` | Plain data, unit tested: progress along the centreline with a sliding search window (continuous, so shortcuts and teleports cannot gain ground), ordered checkpoint gates credited only inside the corridor and taken back when crossed backwards, laps across the line in both directions, lap/sector timing with delta to the best lap, wrong-way detection, reset monitor (flipped, stuck below 1 m/s, off the track: 3 s) with the last valid position, reset placement behind it clear of other cars, standings, sprint goal, DNF timeout. |
| Race loop | `crates/racer-sim/src/race.rs` | `RacePlugin`: countdown with cars held on the brakes (launch control works on the grid), race clock, tracking, resets with 3 s ghosting (solid again only once no car overlaps), the collisions option (full / ghosted first lap / off) through Avian collision layers, standings, finish (lapped cars finish at the line after the winner), DNF 30 s after the winner, results table with gaps, best laps, resets and assists. Circuit, Sprint (the loop opened: grid to a finish just before the grid) and Time Trial (solo). |
| AI drivers | `crates/racer-sim/src/ai.rs` | Drive through `Controls` only, on the same physics and assists as a player. Stanley-style steering at the front axle on the racing line's own heading, curvature feed-forward, yaw-rate damping, a slip-angle limiter (backs the lock off while the fronts slide past their peak). Speed: the driver's own profile (computed at start from the car model at its difficulty's share of the limits) with slope feed-forward and speed feedback, brake pedal mapped through the brake torque, throttle squeezed as the rears near their peak slip, manual gear choice (best-drive gear; an automatic upshifts too early while the throttle comes in). Racecraft: following with a speed cap and time headway, timed overtakes on a free lane beside the car ahead (inside of the next corner first; abandoned if not alongside at the braking point or if the room closes), defending the inside, keeping clear of cars alongside (absolute lateral bounds), spin catching, reversing out of walls, reset requests when recovery fails. Optional mild catch-up (±3 % speed target, never above the car's full-limit profile). |
| Difficulties | `Difficulty::skill` | Novice, Amateur, Pro, Expert, Alien: share of lateral grip / braking / drive used (0.70/0.55/0.80 … 0.995/0.985/1.0), wandering line error (0.45 … 0.02 m), braking-point spread (6 … 0.4 m), mistakes per corner (8 % … 0.2 %: late braking, over-slowing, running wide), overtaking aggression (0.1 … 0.9: how soon it goes for a pass, how close it follows) and contact willingness (0: keeps a wide berth … 1: leans on others and rams back after being hit). |
| Game flow and UI | `crates/racer-game/src/{flow,race_scene,hud,visuals}.rs` | States: main menu → race setup (mode, track, car, AI count 0–7, AI cars same/mixed, difficulty or mixed, laps, collisions, catch-up, ABS/TCS/ESC, steering assist, racing-line assist, gearbox) → race → results → restart or menu, with a pause menu (resume, restart, menu). Keyboard, gamepad and mouse. HUD: position, lap, lap/last/best times, sector times, delta to best, speed, gear, rpm bar, minimap with every car, countdown, wrong-way and off-track warnings. Racing-line assist drawn on the road (green accelerate, red brake). `racer-game race [--car N] [--ai N] [--difficulty D] [--laps N] [--autopilot]` starts a race directly. The M1 driving scene stays as `racer-game drive`. |
| Verification | `racer-capture ai-laps`, `ai-races`, `tools/ai-check.sh`, `tools/plot_race.py` | Headless release-build runs, one simulation per thread (each single-threaded), see below. |

## Problems found while building, and fixes

1. **Racing line hugged the hairpin's inside.** Minimising squared second differences of the
   offset points (a convex QP, the usual shortcut) is biased by the uniform centreline
   parameterisation: on the inside of a hairpin the points bunch up and look straighter than
   they are. The line took the hairpin at 7.5 m radius; reweighting by local spacing got stuck
   in the same fixed point. The solver now minimises the parameterisation-free cost (turning
   angle squared over length) directly; the hairpin is taken wide at 13.7 m and the optimal
   lap got ~2 s faster.
2. **Steering aimed along the centreline.** The AI's heading error used the centreline tangent
   instead of the racing line's own direction, which differs by up to ~9° where the line
   crosses the road between corners: ±2 m tracking errors and 20° front slip at corner entry.
3. **Brake pedal mapping.** Mapping the wanted deceleration through the grip-limited braking
   figure (10.5 m/s²) asked for twice the deceleration at part pedal (the brakes alone can
   give ~20 m/s²); the pedal now maps through the brake torque.
4. **Automatic gearbox and the AI.** With the throttle coming in progressively the automatic
   upshifts at its part-throttle point and the car exits corners a gear too high. The AI now
   shifts manually into the gear with the most drive (as a player may).
5. **Optimistic drive model.** Computing drive force from the torque curve and ratios ignored
   shift gaps, TCS and rotating inertia; the model now uses a full-throttle run on the
   testbed. Out of corners the driven axle carries drive and cornering at once, so the profile
   uses `1 - (a_y / a_y,max)²` of the drive there (braking keeps the ellipse).
6. **Wheels standing on wall tops.** Wheel shape casts hit the 0.4 m wall tops as ground: a car
   brushing the hairpin's inner wall climbed it and rolled over. Walls are on their own
   collision layer that wheel casts ignore (the body still hits them).
7. **AI reversing on the grid.** The stuck check (throttle on, not moving) fired during the
   countdown while the cars are held on the brakes; recovery is off until the start.
8. **Race clock drift.** The race clock was an f32 sum of 1/240 s steps; after ~2 h of
   simulated time each step rounds by ~6 %, so lap times in the 100-lap runs came out several
   seconds too short. The clock is now an f64 sum.
9. **Simulation throughput.** The headless sims ran at ~2000 car-ticks/s with most time in
   thread synchronisation (Bevy's multi-threaded executor and task pools, Avian's parallel
   iteration, with tiny workloads). Single-threaded executors and a one-thread task pool per
   simulation, with simulations run in parallel threads, made it ~4× faster per core.
10. **Racecraft.** The first version followed politely but almost never passed (an Alien sat
   behind a Novice for whole races) and jammed in pile-ups. Changes that held up under 16-race
   samples: keep-clear constraints on absolute lateral offsets (the racing line sweeps across
   the road, so a shift relative to it did not keep cars apart), a pass lane checked against
   every car in the next 35 m, passes abandoned when the room closes or when not alongside at
   the braking point, no passing in the first 5 s. Several other attempts (stricter stopping-
   distance following, pulling forward instead of reversing when boxed in, stronger braking
   gain when following) made contact and resets worse and were reverted.

## Known gaps

- **Racecraft is functional, not good.** AIs follow, pass on free lanes, defend and keep
  clear alongside, but they pass rarely through dense traffic: a fast car starting at the
  back of an 8-car grid usually gains only a few places in a short race, and a car stuck
  behind a much slower one in the same model can lose seconds a lap. Multi-car incidents
  happen in the heavy braking zones (turn 1, the right-hander after the kink, the left at the
  end of the back straight, the downhill right after the overpass) and end in resets in
  traffic (counted below). Overtaking under braking (outbraking), side-by-side cornering rules
  (who owns the inside line) and anticipating the car ahead's braking are the next steps.
- **No ghost car in Time Trial** (PLAN.md lists ghosts with replays, M6); a time trial is a
  solo race with lap and sector times.
- **Track direction, time of day, weather, traffic and damage options** from PLAN.md section 3
  are later milestones; the setup screen offers what M2 asks for.
- **One track and three cars.** The car-class choice is any of the three M1 cars.
- **Optimal line, not optimal lap.** The racing line is minimum-curvature, not lap-time
  optimal, and the speed profile is a quasi-steady point mass; the Alien gets within ~2 % of
  it (M3 slightly more), which is the reference the difficulty spreads are measured against.
- **Free drive is not in the menu**: the M1 driving scene with the test track and skidpad is
  `racer-game drive`, as before.
- **Steering wheels** are supported through the M1 input layer but untested on hardware (no
  device in the container); menus take keyboard, gamepad and mouse.
- **CI** still cannot start jobs on this account (see M0); the workflow now also validates the
  track, captures the menu and race scenes and runs a short AI check.

### Issues found and fixed

1. **Sprint races never finished** (`race.rs`). The goal was only checked on checkpoint and
   line events. The sprint finish (2070.5 m) lies 0.5 m past the last checkpoint (2070.0 m),
   and the next event is the line, where `laps == 1` fails `Goal::Distance`. So in a Sprint no
   car ever finished, and nobody was ever marked DNF. The goal is now checked every step. A new
   headless test, `a_sprint_finishes_at_the_finish_point_between_gates`, fails on the old code
   and passes now.
2. **Lapped finishers ranked by finish time alone** (`rules::standings`). A lapped car takes
   the flag at the line after the winner. If it got there before a lead-lap car, it was placed
   ahead of that car. Finishers now rank by laps, then time.
3. **Laps after the flag** (`race.rs`). Progress keeps counting on the cool-down lap, but the
   results table and the lapped-gap calculation used `progress.laps`. They now use the laps
   timed while racing.
4. **A finished car reset behind the line lost a timed lap**. `apply_resets` fed the undo
   events to `Timing` whatever the car's status. Times now stand once a car has finished.
5. **Line jitter lost the split times** (`Timing`). Crossing the line, backing over it and
   crossing again emptied the checkpoint times, so the next lap had no delta to best. The
   finished lap's split times are now kept and restored on `LapUndone` (new unit test).
6. **Results screen could restart by accident** (`flow.rs`). Space and gamepad South are both
   the handbrake and "activate", and the cursor starts on "Restart race". The results come up
   while the player is often still driving, so one press threw the results away. Keys and pad
   buttons are now ignored for the first second; mouse clicks still work.
7. **Gamepad Start opened the pause menu but could not close it.** Start now acts as "back"
   in the race state only.
8. **The off-track warning always said "reset in 3 s".** It now counts down, through a new
   `ResetMonitor::off_track()` getter.
9. **`ai-laps` did not check that each run completed its laps.** A run cut short by the time
   limit could still pass on its lap times. It now fails.
10. **Docs:** the `profile.rs` module comment still described the old torque-curve drive model.

### Reviewed and found sound

Ordered gates with corridor-gated progress and a sliding window: the hairpin shortcut is
rejected, laps count in both directions, and gates are undone on reverse and on reset. Grid
progress is negative, so crossing the line at the start counts nothing. The f64 race clock
holds f32 precision for lap subtraction even after hours. DNF timing, Time Trial (forced to
0 AI), and 0-7 AI all work. AI honesty holds: the AI writes only `Controls`, with the
player's assists and no physics, grip or power changes. It reads its own telemetry (slip
angles, gear, speed) and the other cars' track position, speed and ghost state. The catch-up
target is capped at the car's full-limit profile. The racing-line solver and speed profile
are correct: corner speed with lift, forward drive pass inside `1 - (a_y/a_y,max)^2`,
backward braking pass inside the ellipse with grade. No reachable panics turned up: road
width is validated to 6-25 m, so every `clamp` has ordered bounds, and `nearest` and
`reset_pose` always have candidates. Collision layers are checked both ways, so ghosting
works from either side.

### Left open

- **The full verification run is still owed:** `tools/ai-check.sh captures/ai`, with 100 solo
  laps per car and difficulty plus 12 races of 10 laps, on the final code (about 2 h). The
  reduced run above stands in for it.
- **Resets in traffic.** First-lap pile-ups at turn 1 and around 955 m reset about 6 % of
  laps in 3-lap races. Short races stress the first lap, so the full 10-lap race run should
  show a lower rate. The real fix is the racecraft work listed under Known gaps.
- With collisions set to "ghosted first lap", a Sprint is ghosted for the whole run, since
  it never completes a lap. This is acceptable, but it is not documented in the setup screen.
- An unknown `racer-game race --difficulty` name silently gives a mixed field.
