# M1 Driving feel: handoff note

Branch `m1-driving-feel`. Built and verified in a GPU-less cloud container (4 cores, 15 GB RAM,
Ubuntu 24.04, Mesa lavapipe) on 2026-10-27. Nothing was pinned or upgraded beyond M0 except the
new crates listed under "Dependencies".

## What was built

| Item | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle model v1 | `crates/racer-dynamics` | Pure Rust (glam, serde, toml; no Bevy). `Vehicle::step(&mut VehicleState, &ChassisState, &Controls, &[Option<WheelContact>; 4], dt) -> StepOutput { force, torque, telemetry }`. `VehicleState` is plain `Copy` data (rollback = copy). 240 Hz chassis step, wheels and drivetrain substepped ×4 inside. |
| Tires | `tire.rs` | Magic Formula per axis with the peak placed at a configured slip (B solved from C, E), combined slip by the similarity method, load-sensitive μ, first-order relaxation (implicit, stable at any speed, a damped spring at standstill), pneumatic-trail aligning torque that fades past the peak, surface grip/drag multipliers (dry/wet asphalt, paint, cobbles, metal, curb, grass, dirt). |
| Suspension | `vehicle.rs` | Spring from ride frequency, bump/rebound damping from damping ratios, progressive bump stop, anti-roll bars, roll-centre (geometric) load transfer through a jacking term in the wheel load; tire forces act at the contact patch. Contacts come from the caller's wheel shape cast (distance + normal + surface). |
| Drivetrain | `drivetrain.rs` | Torque curve, closed-throttle drag, idle governor, rev limiter with hysteresis, flywheel inertia, automatic clutch (launch slip, anti-stall, pedal), launch control (brake + full throttle at rest), gearbox with per-gear final drives (DSG), auto and manual shifting, DCT-style shifts (ignition cut / blip with torque fill), open / clutch-type LSD differentials and an AWD centre differential. Every coupling (clutch, LSDs) is solved implicitly per substep: locked couplings are exactly rigid, slipping ones transmit their capacity. |
| Aero | `vehicle.rs` | Drag through the CoM, lift/downforce per axle. |
| Assists | `assists.rs` | ABS and TCS on wheel-speed (kinematic) slip with a proportional slip band and 10 ms actuator lag; TCS trims engine torque, or clutch torque while pulling away; ESC brakes single wheels against a yaw-rate reference capped by grip. | Car data | `data/cars/<id>/car.toml` | Three cars, published values with per-table source URLs, estimates kept apart under `[estimated]`, reference figures under `[reference]` (value, source, note). |

### Telemetry review

```
$ tools/telemetry.sh captures/telemetry      # 9 CSV + 9 PNG, ~1 min after a release build
```

Plots for all three cars are in `docs/milestones/m1/`. What they show, after the fixes listed
further down:

- **Balance (skidpad, front minus rear slip angle against lateral g):** GTI understeers and
  increasingly so (0.21° at 0.5 g, 0.82° at 0.9 g); M3 mildly (0.20°, 0.58°); 911 closest to
  neutral (0.11°, 0.29°). At the limit all three push wide on a saturated front axle (front
  slip ~10° against ~3° rear). That ordering is what the layouts and weight distributions
  predict. (Figures from the post-review plots; the skidpad run now reaches the limit.)
- **Weight transfer:** outside wheels load up in corners (inside rear of the GTI drops below 1 kN
  at 0.95 g, the classic FWD three-wheel stance); front loads rise and rears drop under braking
  (front axle static to braking: GTI 9.2 → 12.4 kN, M3 9.4 → 12.8 kN, 911 5.6 → 9.2 kN), the
  reverse under acceleration; the 911 carries most of its load on the rear throughout.
- **Gear changes:** launch control holds launch rpm, then clean upshifts near the limiter with a
  short rpm drop each (DCT fill), and only downshifts under braking; the governed cars sit on 250 km/h.
- **Slip:** a short wheelspin peak at launch trimmed by TCS to about 1.2× peak slip; ABS holds
  braking slip near the peak to a standstill; lap slip angles stay within 5° except at the crest
  landing.
- **Crest:** at ~100 km/h all cars take off (~0.5 s) and land with 35–72 kN peak wheel loads and
  a short rebound, then carry on (the lateral-g spikes in the lap plots are the landing).

## Problems found while building, and fixes

1. **ABS limit cycles.** An integral ABS controller on the tire's relaxed slip oscillated
   between locking and full release; replaced by a proportional slip band with actuator lag, and
   later switched to kinematic (wheel-speed sensor) slip because the relaxed slip lags more and
   more as speed drops.
2. **Launch bog.** Dumping the clutch at the launch speed spun the wheels and dragged the engine
   to idle. The clutch now carries about the engine's torque while slipping, TCS trims the clutch
   instead of the engine while pulling away, and launch control holds the launch speed.
3. **Ghost collisions.** Built from 2 m boxes, the road made the chassis collider catch box end
   faces at speed (a ~600 g stop after the crest landing). The road is now one trimesh with
   internal-edge fixing.
4. **Noisy contact normals.** Parry's cylinder cast returned normals tilted by up to 13° on flat
   boxes, buzzing the suspension. The cast still finds the contact; a short ray at the contact
   point gives the normal, and the distance is solved exactly against that plane.
5. **Accelerometer blips.** Single-step solver blips (~2 mm/s, invisible) showed as 20 % spikes
   in velocity-difference acceleration; the logged acceleration is low-passed (20 ms).

## Known gaps

- **Camber** (thrust and camber/toe curves vs travel), **ride-height-sensitive aero**, active
  aero, slipstream and torque vectoring are not modelled. The tire has no temperature or wear
  (out of scope per the plan). Wheels have no unsprung mass (quasi-static vertical).
- **Steering-wheel hardware** is untested (no device); the SDL path builds, initialises and is
  covered through the trait and mock only.
- **Quarter-mile test** from PLAN.md section 4 is not implemented (not in the M1 task list).
- **Reference figures:** braking distances are converted from mph tests; the M3 weight
  distribution and GTI limiter are estimates. The M3 front/rear grip split and brake balance are
  fits, not measurements.
- The **autopilot** is a test driver (a little brake chatter and steering dither at the hairpin);
  real AI drivers are M2.
- **Telemetry and captures** are not run by `cargo test` (they need a release build / Xvfb);
  `tools/telemetry.sh` and `tools/capture.sh` are the entry points, and the CI capture job runs
  both. CI itself still cannot start jobs on this account (see M0), so the first green CI run
  is still owed.
- `tools/plot_telemetry.py` needs a Python that can import the distro's matplotlib; this
  container's default `python3` is 3.13 while Ubuntu's package targets 3.12, so
  `tools/telemetry.sh` picks whichever works.

### Physics review

Conventions check out: body frame +X right, +Y up, −Z forward throughout; steering positive
left inside the model (input +1 = right); slip and force signs, the aligning-torque sign (a tire
sliding right returns the wheel), Ackermann (inside wheel more), ESC wheel choice (outside front
on oversteer, inside rear on understeer), inertia axes (X pitch, Y yaw, Z roll) in both the
testbed and the Avian `AngularInertia`. The clutch and LSD couplings are solved implicitly and
correctly (rederived the lock torque); relaxation is integrated implicitly; the tire stays
inside the friction ellipse. The reference maneuvers are honest: the scripted drivers are plain
feedback laws with no per-car cases, and the Avian path reproduces the same numbers (below).
`VehicleState` is `Copy` and the step uses no hidden state, now backed by a replay test.

### Issues found and fixed

1. **Load transfer ~17 % short** (`vehicle.rs`, physics). Tire forces were applied at roll-centre
   height with nothing carrying the rest of their moment, so the overturning moment was
   F (h − h_rc) instead of F h. Measured in the testbed: steady cornering moment from the wheel
   loads 0.82–0.84 of m a h, braking front gain 0.82–0.84 of m a h / L. Fix: the tire force acts
   at the contact patch, and each wheel load carries the linkage's jacking force
   −F_lat h_rc / x_wheel (from the last step's lateral force, plain data in `WheelState`), so the
   total is m a h / track while the springs still carry only the share above the roll centre
   (body roll unchanged). Afterwards 0.98–0.99 (lateral, the rest is the tilted accelerometer)
   and 0.96–1.01 (braking). Less grip followed from load sensitivity, so μ_lat was refitted
   (GTI/M3/911 1.17/1.22/1.24 → 1.22/1.27/1.30), and the M3's rear μ_long fit came down from
   1.45 to 1.35 now that its rears gain their full load under acceleration.
2. **Aligning torque never reached the chassis** (`vehicle.rs`): computed for force feedback but
   not applied. Now added to the chassis torque.
3. **Driveline created energy on the overrun** (`drivetrain.rs`): the efficiency scaled the
   wheel-side torque the same way in both directions, so under engine braking the wheels gave up
   only η of what the engine absorbed. When the wheels drive the engine they now supply 1/η.
4. **Fuzz energy check could not fail** (`tests/fuzz.rs`): the bound was initial + 1.05 × peak
   power × t (~23 MJ after a minute). It now counts wheel and flywheel energy and must stay
   within the engine's actual positive work +2 % +20 kJ (a bound of 0.9 × that work, no margin, fails).
5. **Telemetry skidpad never reached the limit** (`racer-capture/src/telemetry.rs`): the ramp
   started at 10 m/s and hit the 90 s cap at ~0.95 g with every car still on the line, so the
   handoff's "near the limit" balance remarks were not backed by the plots. The ramp now starts
   at 14 m/s; assists stay on for the run-up from rest only (the 911 spun on a full-throttle,
   assists-off start). All cars now run wide at their limit before the cap.
6. **Automatic upshifts under hard braking** (`drivetrain.rs`): with the throttle closed the
   upshift point drops to its part-throttle value, so a stop from 120 km/h went 3-4-5-6 before
   downshifting. Upshifts now wait for the brake to be released.
7. **Driving captures ran the car at 64 Hz** (`racer-sim/src/vehicle.rs`): the capture scene
   added `VehiclePlugin` without `SimPlugin`, so the fixed step was Bevy's default 64 Hz, not
   240 Hz. After fix 1 the parked car settled into a step-rate limit cycle at that rate (the HUD
